Beginner guides

Start small. Pick one audience and solve one real problem.

Service

Offer a small freelance service

Examples include short video edits, document formatting, simple spreadsheet cleanup, or proofreading you are qualified to do.

  • Make a sample with your own or fictional material.
  • Offer a clearly defined result and delivery time.
  • Ask potential customers what they actually need.

Free test: Talk to five relevant prospects before buying tools.

Digital product

Create a useful download

A checklist, planner, tracker, or template can be sold repeatedly if it solves a real problem and is easy to use.

  • Choose a narrow audience.
  • Include instructions and a worked example.
  • Get feedback before listing it.

Free test: Offer a preview to likely users and ask what they would change.

Content

Publish genuinely helpful guides

Build an information site around a topic you can research and explain well. Traffic may take time and is never guaranteed.

  • Answer a specific question thoroughly.
  • Check facts, dates, and prices.
  • Use your own examples and experience.

Free test: Publish one excellent guide, then measure impressions and feedback.

Affiliate

Recommend products transparently

Affiliate programs may pay a commission on qualifying purchases. You must be accepted and follow the program's terms.

  • Evaluate products honestly.
  • Explain limitations and alternatives.
  • Disclose commissions clearly near links.

Free test: Research program eligibility and fees before applying.

AI-assisted work

Use AI as a helper, not a shortcut

AI can help brainstorm, outline, and draft, but the final result should be reviewed for accuracy, originality, usefulness, and rights.

  • Check claims against reliable sources.
  • Remove repetitive filler.
  • Never invent reviews, results, or credentials.

Free test: Improve one real deliverable and compare it with your original.

Research

Check demand before committing

Search interest is a clue, not proof that people will pay. Look at customer questions, competitors, and actual conversations.

  • Compare time periods and related searches.
  • Look for problems people already spend money to solve.
  • Test with a small sample.

Free test: Ask five target users about their current workaround.

Your 7-day low-cost test

Use this checklist for one idea at a time.

  1. Day 1 — Choose a specific customer. Write down who has the problem and what the problem is.
  2. Day 2 — Research the problem. Review search trends, existing solutions, fees, and competition.
  3. Day 3 — Make one sample. Use free tools and your own or properly licensed material.
  4. Day 4 — Get feedback. Ask a few relevant people clear, non-pushy questions.
  5. Day 5 — Improve the offer. Explain exactly what is included, what it costs, and what is not included.
  6. Day 6 — Test interest honestly. Share with relevant communities only where promotion is allowed. No spam or fake engagement.
  7. Day 7 — Review evidence. Record real inquiries, feedback, costs, and any sales. Continue, revise, or stop based on what happened.
